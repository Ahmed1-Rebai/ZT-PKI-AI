# M4 — Anomaly engine: baseline results (CIC-IDS2017, experiment E1)

Generated by `ai/train.py`; raw numbers in [`metrics.json`](metrics.json),
artefact hashes in [`../models/manifest.json`](../models/manifest.json).

## Data and protocol

- **Dataset:** CIC-IDS2017 `MachineLearningCSV` — verified against the published
  totals (2,830,743 flows, 2,273,097 benign, 15 labels).
- **Deduplication:** 850,380 exact duplicates removed → **1,980,363 unique flows**
  (336,739 attacks). Without this, identical rows land in both train and test.
- **Features:** 47 canonical flow features (`ai/schema.py`), mappable from
  CICFlowMeter *and* NFStream, rates recomputed, `log1p` + standardisation.
- **Split:** stratified by label — train 1,188,219 / val 396,072 / test 396,072.
- **No leakage:** IF and AE fit on benign *train* only; RF on train; normalisation,
  stacking, calibration and thresholds fitted on *validation*; every number below
  is on the untouched *test* split.

### What drives detections (SHAP, Random Forest, test attacks)

`bwd_ps_std`, `ps_std`, `bwd_ps_mean`, `bwd_ps_max`, `ps_max`, `bwd_bytes` —
packet-size statistics of the server→client direction dominate.

## Reading these numbers honestly

1. **CIC-IDS2017 is optimistic.** Its attacks are generated by a few tools and are
   separable largely by packet-size statistics (the SHAP ranking shows this). A
   near-perfect score here does **not** mean the same on another network.
2. **The real test is E2** (train on CIC → test on *our* lab traffic captured with
   NFStream). The shared schema exists precisely for that. Expect a drop; the
   lab-trained model and the UEBA windows (E3) are the answer to it.
3. **Isolation Forest is weak alone** at a 1 % FPR budget (F1 0.0035) despite a
   fair ROC-AUC: at flow level most CIC attacks sit inside benign density. It is
   kept for *unknown* attacks, and stacking learns how much to trust it.
4. **Bot and infiltration** are the weak families (low-volume, benign-looking
   flows) — exactly what per-entity behaviour windows (UEBA, E3) target.
5. The fused score is a **calibrated probability**, which is what the PDP needs:
   it feeds the trust score as the anomaly term.
